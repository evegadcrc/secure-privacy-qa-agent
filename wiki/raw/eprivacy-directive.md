# ePrivacy Directive — Article 5(3)

## The Legal Text

Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC (as amended by Directive 2009/136/EC) states:

> "Member States shall ensure that the storing of information, or the gaining of access to information already stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user is only allowed on condition that the subscriber or user concerned has given his or her consent, having been provided with clear and comprehensive information, in accordance with Directive 95/46/EC [now GDPR], inter alia, about the purposes of the processing."

## The Strictly Necessary Exemption

The consent requirement does NOT apply to storage/access that is:
1. For the sole purpose of carrying out the transmission of a communication over an electronic communications network, OR
2. Is **strictly necessary** in order for the provider of an information society service explicitly requested by the subscriber or user to provide the service

## Interaction with GDPR

The ePrivacy Directive is **lex specialis** (specific law) to the GDPR's **lex generalis** (general law):

- **ePrivacy Article 5(3)** governs the **act of placing/reading cookies** on the user's device (terminal equipment access)
- **GDPR** governs the **subsequent processing of personal data** collected via those cookies
- The consent standard required by ePrivacy (post-2009 amendment) is now defined by reference to the GDPR's definition of consent in Article 4(11)
- This means the full GDPR consent standard (freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, affirmative action) applies to cookie placement
- **Critical:** There is NO legitimate interest basis for placing non-essential cookies. Consent is the ONLY legal basis under ePrivacy Article 5(3)

## What Counts as "Strictly Necessary" (Exempt from Consent)

Based on Article 29 Working Party Opinion 04/2012:

### Exempt (no consent needed)
- User-input cookies (session ID, shopping cart, form data)
- Authentication/session cookies
- User security cookies (authentication abuse detection)
- Multimedia content player session cookies
- Load-balancing session cookies
- UI customization cookies (language preference) — limited scope
- First-party analytics cookies used ONLY for aggregate statistical purposes with limited retention and no cross-site tracking (nuanced position — CNIL specifically allows this under certain conditions)

### NOT Exempt (consent required)
- Third-party analytics cookies (including Google Analytics)
- Advertising/marketing cookies
- Social media tracking cookies
- Cross-site tracking cookies of any kind
- First-party analytics shared with third parties
- Any cookie used for profiling or behavioral advertising

## ePrivacy Regulation (Proposed Replacement)

The ePrivacy Regulation was proposed in 2017 to replace the Directive but has been stalled in EU legislative process. As of 2026, the Directive (as implemented by member states) remains in force. The proposed Regulation would:
- Directly apply across the EU (no national implementation needed)
- Cover more technologies (not just cookies)
- Potentially align more closely with GDPR enforcement mechanisms

## Member State Implementations

Each EU member state has implemented Article 5(3) differently:
- **Germany**: TTDSG Section 25 (2021)
- **France**: CNIL guidelines and enforcement
- **UK** (post-Brexit): PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations)
- **Spain**: LSSI
- **Italy**: Garante guidelines
