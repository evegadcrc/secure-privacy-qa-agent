# Germany — TTDSG and DSK Cookie Guidance

## TTDSG (Telekommunikation-Telemedien-Datenschutz-Gesetz)

Enacted December 2021, the TTDSG is Germany's national implementation of ePrivacy Article 5(3).

### Section 25 TTDSG

Codifies the principle: consent required for storing/accessing information on end devices. Exception only for technically necessary operations.

This was significant because Germany previously lacked a clear national transposition of Article 5(3), creating legal uncertainty. The TTDSG resolved this.

## DSK (Conference of Independent Data Protection Authorities) Guidance

The German Conference of Independent Data Protection Authorities published guidance affirming:

1. **No pre-ticked boxes**: All non-essential cookie categories must default to off
2. **Reject must be equally accessible as accept**: Same visual prominence, same number of clicks
3. **Dark patterns in cookie banners are non-compliant**: Asymmetric styling, hidden reject, misleading language
4. **Cookie walls are not permitted**: Germany takes a stricter position than some other member states — cookie walls are generally considered non-compliant regardless of alternatives offered

## Federated DPA System

Germany has 17 DPAs (one federal + 16 state-level). Key DPAs for cookie enforcement:

### Bavarian DPA (BayLDA)
- Particularly active in cookie enforcement
- Conducted coordinated audits of cookie banners across hundreds of websites
- Published detailed guidance on compliant banner design
- Has issued formal orders requiring website modifications

### Hamburg DPA
- Active in large tech company enforcement
- Google, Facebook cases often involve Hamburg

## Planet49 Origin

The Planet49 case originated in German courts (Bundesgerichtshof referred questions to the CJEU). Germany was therefore the first member state to directly apply the ruling's principles. German courts and DPAs have been strict in their interpretation since.

## Key Principles from German Enforcement

1. **Strictest interpretation**: Germany generally takes the strictest reading of ePrivacy/GDPR consent requirements
2. **Cookie walls banned**: Unlike some member states that allow "pay or consent" models, Germany generally rejects this
3. **Analytics consent required**: No broad exception for analytics cookies — consent is needed
4. **Active enforcement**: Multiple DPAs conducting systematic audits
