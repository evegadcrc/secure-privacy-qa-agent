# ICO Cookie Guidance

The UK applies essentially the same consent standard as the EU through the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) + UK GDPR (post-Brexit).

## Core Guidance

The ICO states:
> "You must tell people clearly what cookies you want to set and what they do. You must get the user's consent before you set any cookies that are not strictly necessary."

### Key Positions

1. **Implied consent is NOT valid**: "By continuing to browse you agree" is explicitly rejected
2. **Prominent, specific, opt-in consent mechanisms** required
3. **Reject must be available**: Cookie banners that only offer "Accept" and "Settings" (no reject) are non-compliant
4. **Equal effort**: Consent mechanisms requiring more effort to reject than accept are non-compliant
5. **Legitimate interest for advertising cookies**: NOT valid for cookies under PECR — consent is the only basis

### ICO Opinion (November 2023)

Called out common non-compliant practices:
- Cookie banners that only offer "Accept" and "Settings" (no reject)
- Consent mechanisms requiring more effort to reject than accept
- "Legitimate interest" claimed for advertising cookies
- Pre-ticked checkboxes for non-essential categories

## PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003)

PECR is the UK implementation of the ePrivacy Directive. Regulation 6 covers cookies:
- Consent required before storing/accessing information on a user's device
- Exception only for cookies strictly necessary to provide a service the user has requested
- The consent standard is now defined by UK GDPR

## Enforcement Approach

The ICO has been less aggressive than CNIL in terms of fine amounts but has:
- Issued enforcement notices and reprimands to organizations
- Published detailed guidance and audit tools
- Conducted sector sweeps of cookie compliance
- Focused on public sector and large commercial sites

## Differences from EU Post-Brexit

- UK GDPR mirrors EU GDPR in substance but is governed by UK courts
- PECR is UK domestic law (not directly tied to EU ePrivacy Directive updates)
- ICO enforcement is independent of EU DPA coordination
- Adequacy decision allows data flows between UK and EU (currently in force)
- Any future ePrivacy Regulation would not apply to the UK
